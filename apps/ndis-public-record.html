A citizen's guide to the published evidence

What the records show. And what they don't.

Public records reveal enforcement actions, unexplained changes and unusual accounting patterns. They can help us ask better questions. They cannot, by themselves, prove that a provider committed fraud.

The register changes over time

Actions can appear—and disappear

The Commission releases dated copies of its current compliance register. Comparing them shows changes that no single copy can reveal.

What this meansA row was present in one published extract and absent from every later extract checked, even though its own end date had not arrived.
What this does not meanIt does not prove a cover-up or an error. A successful appeal, a correction or another lawful change could explain it.

A register hit is not one simple thing

Most compliance notices came in audit batches

The action type and the Commission's narrative matter more than the mere presence of a provider's name.

93%

cite the same audit obligation

1,977 of 2,118 compliance notices cite section 73J. That is an audit requirement, not necessarily participant harm or fraud.

91%

fell on just seven effective dates

This batch pattern means a compliance notice may say more about a regulatory campaign than about an individual provider.

What this meansRead “banning order”, “revocation” and “compliance notice” as different kinds of action. Read the narrative to learn what the Commission actually published.
What this does not meanA compliance notice is not proof of fraud. Conversely, no register entry is not an all-clear.
Providers' own charity filings

Accounting shapes that deserve a closer look

These comparisons use two years of self-reported revenue, employee expenses and staffing. They identify unanswered questions, not wrongdoing.

What this meansThe numbers reported by a charity changed in a way that may justify reading its full filing and asking how its service-delivery model works.
What this does not meanContractors, labour-hire arrangements, restructures, aged-care programs and simple data-entry errors can create the same shapes lawfully.

Relationships hidden in prose

Separate rows can describe one connected case

The structured register does not link people and organisations. Its written narratives sometimes do. Every live connection below is accompanied by the Commission's own words.

What this meansThe same organisation is literally named across more than one subject's enforcement narrative, or the Commission explicitly describes a role such as director or key personnel.
What this does not meanA mention is not automatically adverse. An organisation can be named as an employer, context, affected provider or bystander. The quotation determines the relationship.

A simple evidence ladder

How to read this responsibly

The app keeps four kinds of information separate so that an unanswered question does not quietly become an accusation.

Published statement

What the Commission or a provider wrote. Strong evidence of what was published, though not necessarily a court-tested fact.

Arithmetic pattern

A comparison or ratio over published numbers. It can locate an unusual shape but cannot explain why it exists.

Missing information

A row disappeared or money is not itemised in the registers checked. Absence creates a question, not an answer.

External reporting

News or legal-index material may add context. Names can collide, so the original document must be opened before relying on it.

Coverage and method in plain English

This work covers the NDIS Commission's published compliance-action extracts from February 2026 onward and ACNC-registered charities with disability programs in their filings. It does not cover every NDIS provider, unregistered operators or a complete per-provider NDIS payment ledger, because those data are not public. Register changes are computed without a model. Accounting screens are arithmetic. The connection finder uses a model to propose names, then drops any name that does not literally appear in the Commission's text.

This app reports public records and arithmetic over them. It does not determine fraud.
